# Research note: AI Video Editing for Beginners: From Raw Footage to Published Clips

- Backlog ID: 10
- Primary keyword: `ai video editing beginners`
- Checked: 2026-07-31
- Dominant intent: beginner tutorial and conceptual orientation
- Reader: someone with raw talking-head, podcast, lesson, demo, or interview footage who wants to publish a first useful clip without mastering a professional editor first

## Search-result pattern

Ranking pages often describe AI video editing as a shortcut around the learning curve, then list tools and features. Official guides are more grounded: Adobe starts with cutting, trimming, text, and audio; Descript explains that transcript edits change the media while retaining a timeline; Adobe's Premiere documentation separates transcript-based rough cutting from caption work and final export.

Several weak pages blur AI generation, AI-assisted editing, and full automation. They also claim near-perfect transcription or one-click “professional” results without evidence. Beginners need a mental model for the edit, not a shopping list.

## Questions the article must answer

- What does AI video editing actually do?
- Which editing terms must a beginner understand?
- What should happen before importing footage?
- How do transcript editing and a timeline relate?
- What needs manual review?
- How do captions, audio, framing, and export fit together?
- What is a small first project that teaches the whole workflow?

## Gaps to beat

- Tool lists dominate the query and age quickly.
- AI generation is confused with editing existing footage.
- Beginners are told to “polish” without a testable review order.
- Export is treated as a download button, not a delivery check.
- Rights, backups, transcript errors, and versioning are skipped.

## Non-commodity contribution

Teach a **five-pass edit** where each pass has one question: story, boundaries, comprehension, frame, delivery. The constraint reduces beginner overload and makes AI suggestions auditable. Include a worked talking-head example that shows how a transcript candidate becomes a complete clip without inventing results or timings.

## Product-truth notes

Vidrial is suitable for an authorised long-form-to-short-form example: Available moment discovery, prompt search, complete-thought detection, transcript editing, caption correction, reviewable edit plans, timeline rearrangement, and MP4 export. Animated caption presets and long-silence removal are Beta. Do not promise filler-word removal, automatic subject tracking, multi-speaker layouts, B-roll, voiceover, translation, SRT/VTT export, or NLE XML export; those are Coming soon.

## Risks for review

- Avoid presenting one interface as the universal editing model.
- Do not call AI output finished or imply that transcription is reliably error-free.
- Keep the tutorial focused on editing existing authorised footage, not generating synthetic video.
- Recheck Vidrial Beta and Coming soon labels before publication.
