# Research note: How to Turn Long Videos Into Shorts With AI

- Backlog ID: 2
- Primary query: `long video to shorts`
- Checked: 2026-07-31
- Dominant intent: Complete a repeatable long-to-short workflow, not merely find a tool.
- Intended reader: A creator or small team repurposing its own podcast, webinar, tutorial, interview, course, or product recording.

## Query and result review

Searches reviewed included `how to turn long video into shorts with AI`, `long video to shorts workflow`, and current official documentation for YouTube Shorts, Adobe Express Clip Maker, Descript Create clips, Vizard, and OpusClip.

Many ranking pages reduce the workflow to upload, generate, and publish. That skips the decisions that determine whether a short stands alone: editorial brief, source suitability, candidate triage, boundary repair, visual crop, caption correction, platform check, and final rights/context review. Adobe's official workflow explicitly includes review, trim/extend, crop correction, and further editing, which supports a review-first approach.

- Community discussions point to the same recurring repair work: the selected range starts after the real setup, ends before the payoff, crops the wrong speaker, or leaves caption timing errors. Use these as qualitative prompts for the checklist only.

## Consensus, disagreement, and cautions

Consensus: AI is useful for discovery and first-draft construction; human review is still needed before publication. Spoken, topic-dense recordings usually give selection systems more useful material than sparse or mostly visual footage.

Disagreement: vendor pages equate more generated clips with more value. A smaller set with complete ideas can reduce review time and protect context. Platform lengths are ceilings, not editorial targets.

Rights caution: the workflow must start from authorised source media. A public URL is not proof of permission. YouTube's longer-Shorts page also documents that active third-party Content ID claims can block Shorts over one minute globally.

## Non-commodity contribution

Use a **context budget** during candidate review. Score each candidate 0, 1, or 2 for missing setup: 0 means it stands alone; 1 means a short title or one preceding sentence repairs it; 2 means the viewer needs substantial earlier context. Publish 0s, selectively repair 1s, and return 2s to the source rather than forcing them into shorts.
