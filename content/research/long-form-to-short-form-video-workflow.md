# Research note: The Complete Long-Form to Short-Form Video Workflow

- Backlog ID: 9
- Primary keyword: `video repurposing workflow`
- Checked: 2026-07-31
- Dominant intent: step-by-step informational workflow with light commercial investigation
- Reader: a creator or content team turning authorised podcasts, webinars, interviews, tutorials, or long videos into a reliable queue of short clips

## Search-result pattern

Most ranking pages use a linear “upload, let AI find clips, add captions, publish” outline. Stronger results acknowledge that a clip needs a hook, mobile framing, and review. Official documentation provides the factual boundaries: YouTube currently classifies eligible square or vertical uploads of up to three minutes as Shorts; Descript exposes prompt-guided clipping with editable results; Adobe treats export as a separate settings and preview stage; W3C requires synchronized captions for prerecorded audio in synchronized media.

The weak point is selection discipline. Pages often claim a fixed number of clips per hour of source or encourage publishing every generated option. Neither rule accounts for source density, repetition, context, audience, or rights.

## Questions the article must answer

- What should be decided before uploading the source?
- How do you identify a moment that can stand alone?
- How much context should a short retain?
- When should a clip be rewritten rather than extracted?
- What changes when landscape footage becomes vertical?
- How should captions, sound, export, packaging, and publishing be checked?
- How do performance results improve the next source recording?

## Gaps to beat

- Fixed yield claims that encourage weak clips.
- No separation between candidate discovery and editorial acceptance.
- Little attention to rights, source quality, transcript correction, or version control.
- “Optimize for each platform” without a concrete delivery checklist.
- Analytics advice that looks only at views and never ties results back to a hypothesis.

## Non-commodity contribution

Introduce a **clip acceptance contract** with four checks: one-sentence promise, context tax, visual survivability, and destination job. A candidate proceeds only if the editor can state what the viewer gets, add the missing context without rebuilding the whole segment, preserve the important visual information in the target frame, and name the clip's job. Track accepted and rejected candidates in a clip ledger so the team learns which source structures yield reusable moments.

## Risks for review

- The YouTube three-minute rule is time-sensitive and must be rechecked before publication.
- Do not imply all destination platforms share YouTube's classification rules.
- Avoid a universal clip count; source density determines useful yield.
- Direct publishing and scheduling are permission- and connector-dependent; the article should not imply universal one-click distribution.
