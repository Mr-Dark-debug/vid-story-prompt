For one production file that can become a TikTok, Instagram Reel, and YouTube Short, start with a 1080 x 1920, 9:16 MP4. Keep text clear of interface overlays, use readable captions, and export cleanly without another platform's watermark. That is a practical cross-platform default, not a claim that every platform accepts only that size.

The limits differ, and the differences become confusing when articles mix organic posts, ads, and boost eligibility in the same table. This comparison keeps those contexts separate.

## Current organic format comparison

The table below summarizes official platform documentation checked on July 31, 2026.

| Platform | Current documented organic or creator upload | Useful production default | Important caveat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok | TikTok Studio browser upload: MP4 or WebM, at least 720 x 1280, under 10 GB, up to 30 minutes | 1080 x 1920, 9:16 MP4 | Mobile features, duration choices, and availability can vary by account, region, and rollout; confirm the composer |
| Instagram Reels | Aspect ratio 1.91:1 through 9:16; at least 30 FPS and 720 pixels; editor supports Reels up to 20 minutes | 1080 x 1920, 9:16 MP4 | Instagram says Reels longer than three minutes will not be recommended to new audiences; boosted Reels have separate rules |
| YouTube Shorts | Square or vertical video up to three minutes is classified as a Short for standard channels under the current rules | 1080 x 1920, 9:16 MP4 | Shorts longer than one minute with an active Content ID claim are blocked globally; music availability and limits vary |

The "production default" column is an editorial recommendation for interoperability. The other columns summarize official rules. Do not present the recommendation as a platform requirement.

## TikTok: distinguish Studio uploads from ad specifications

TikTok's current Creator tools help page says TikTok Studio in a web browser supports MP4 or WebM uploads, requires 720 x 1280 or higher, allows files under 10 GB, and supports video up to 30 minutes. It also notes that some features are not available everywhere.

The mobile app may offer different creation controls or limits depending on account and rollout. The safest operational check is the live composer used by the publishing account.

TikTok for Business currently recommends 9:16 vertical video for in-feed ads. Its June 2026 specification permits additional orientations for some non-Spark placements, gives a vertical minimum of 540 x 960, and supports ad video up to 10 minutes. Those are ad rules. They should not be copied into an organic-video table as though TikTok has one universal ten-minute maximum.

For creators, 1080 x 1920 gives a clean full-screen vertical master with room for reframing and captions. Duration should follow the idea: a 22-second demonstration and a two-minute explanation can both be valid if each earns its length.

## Instagram Reels: upload length is not recommendation length

Instagram's help center currently says users can record and edit Reels up to 20 minutes. The same page says Reels over three minutes will not be recommended to new audiences. That difference matters when the goal is discovery.

Instagram accepts Reels between 1.91:1 and 9:16, at a minimum of 30 FPS and 720 pixels. A landscape file can therefore be accepted, but acceptance does not mean it will use the full vertical viewing area. For a Reel designed from long-form source, 9:16 is usually the cleanest new edit.

Boosting is another context. Instagram currently requires a Reel to be under 90 seconds and full-screen 9:16 for boosting. Certain creative elements, including copyrighted music, GIFs, interactive stickers, or third-party camera filters, can also make a Reel ineligible. A two-minute organic Reel may be valid and still not qualify for that paid workflow.

Read the [long-video-to-Instagram-Reels workflow](/blog/turn-long-videos-into-instagram-reels) for selection, framing, caption, and cover decisions beyond the file specification.

## YouTube Shorts: three minutes, with a rights caveat

YouTube's current guidance classifies square or vertical videos up to three minutes as Shorts for standard channels when uploaded under the current post-October-15-2024 rules. The creator help pages also describe Shorts as up to three minutes and indicate a maximum upload resolution of 1080p in the Shorts creation context.

The important exception is music and Content ID. YouTube says a Short over one minute with an active Content ID claim will be blocked globally until the claim is resolved. That can prevent playback, recommendation, and monetization. Music from YouTube's libraries can have duration limits that depend on the track.

Do not assume that a song cleared inside another platform is cleared for YouTube. Keep a dialogue-only or separately licensed master when the same edit will travel.

Square video can qualify as a Short, but 9:16 fills the vertical player more naturally. A landscape upload does not become a Short merely because it is brief.

## Resolution, frame rate, codec, and file choice

For a shared master, use:

- **canvas:** 1080 x 1920 pixels;
- **aspect ratio:** 9:16;
- **container:** MP4;
- **video codec:** H.264 is a widely compatible delivery choice;
- **audio:** AAC in the MP4;
- **frame rate:** match the source where practical rather than creating frames without a reason;
- **color:** standard SDR delivery unless the entire capture-to-publish path is intentionally HDR.

Do not upscale a soft 480p source and call it high quality. The larger file will contain the same missing detail. Use the original recording, crop conservatively, and preview platform compression.

Variable frame-rate phone or screen recordings can drift against separately recorded audio in some editing workflows. Transcode to a stable editing format when sync problems appear, then export the platform master.

Avoid repeated downloads and re-uploads. Every social platform compresses video; downloading the published copy to feed another service adds another generation of loss and may carry a watermark.

## One master does not mean one finished post

The clean 9:16 master is a production asset. Each platform version may still need a different ending, caption position, on-platform text, cover, or call to action.

TikTok viewers may see dense right-side controls and a changing caption block. Reels adds its own lower interface and profile-grid cover crop. Shorts has its own title behavior and lets a creator connect a related video, while ordinary URLs in Shorts descriptions and comments are not clickable under YouTube's current linking rules.

Keep the story the same but adapt the packaging. Do not tell a Shorts viewer to "tap the link in the TikTok bio," and do not leave a TikTok-specific follow animation inside a Reel.

## Captions and interface safe zones

No single permanent pixel rectangle is guaranteed across TikTok, Reels, and Shorts. Interface labels change with device, language, caption length, ad or organic context, and product updates. TikTok's own ad documentation explicitly says its safe zone varies with dimensions, caption length, and additional formats.

Start with the essential captions in a central reading area, away from the top edge, bottom metadata, and right-side control stack. Then upload an unpublished or private test and inspect the actual player. Follow the [short-form safe-zone guide](/blog/safe-zones-for-tiktok-reels-and-shorts) instead of copying an unverified overlay.

Keep a clean caption-free master and a project with editable text. If an interface changes, you can reposition captions without reconstructing the video.

## Length should follow the promise

Platform maximums answer "Will the file fit?" They do not answer "Will the explanation work?"

Choose duration by the content:

- a visual result may need only the setup and transformation;
- a technical correction may need a definition, example, and limitation;
- a podcast story may require enough time for the listener to trust the conclusion;
- a product walkthrough needs pauses for the viewer to follow the interface.

If viewers leave before the payoff, remove duplicated setup or choose a more compact idea. Do not speed the voice until it sounds unnatural simply to meet an inherited 60-second habit.

## Cross-platform export checklist

Before publishing, verify that:

- the project uses an original, high-quality source;
- the shared master is 9:16 and no wider-platform crop hides essential content;
- the duration satisfies each intended organic, boosted, or ad context;
- music rights cover every destination;
- captions were checked in each live player;
- the file contains no other platform's watermark or interface;
- covers, titles, descriptions, and calls to action are platform-specific;
- a clean master and editable caption project are archived;
- time-sensitive specifications were rechecked before a major campaign.
