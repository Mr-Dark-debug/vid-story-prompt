The best AI podcast clip maker depends on where your bottleneck sits. Choose OpusClip or Vizard when you mainly want an automatic shortlist from finished video. Choose Riverside when recording, separate tracks, editing, and repurposing should live together. Choose Descript when transcript correction and precise text-led editing matter more than a large automatic batch. Choose quso.ai when clipping and multi-platform scheduling need to share one workspace.

This comparison is based on current official pricing, product, and help documentation. We did not run a controlled hands-on test, so “best” here means best documented fit for a workflow—not a performance ranking.

**Pricing and features checked on 2026-07-31.** Vendors change credits, plan names, limits, and promotional prices frequently. Recheck the linked official page before purchase.

## How to compare podcast clippers

A polished demo clip hides most of the work that determines whether a tool fits a weekly show. Score the workflow in this order.

### Selection recall and control

Give every tool the same representative episode, then mark five to ten moments you already consider usable. How many appear somewhere in its shortlist? If the tool misses one, can you retrieve it by topic, transcript selection, or timestamp without rebuilding the project?

This is more informative than comparing each product's “virality” number. A proprietary score may help order a vendor's own candidates, but it is not a shared measurement across products and cannot guarantee reach.

### Boundary quality

Inspect the first and final five seconds of each suggestion. Does the clip begin after the subject was named? Does it end before the qualification? Can you extend into the source transcript or timeline, or are you forced to accept the automatic window?

### Speaker framing

Podcast video exposes weak auto-reframing quickly. Review speaker changes, crosstalk, reaction shots, screen shares, and guests who sit far apart. Look for manual correction and stable split layouts, not just the claim “auto reframe.”

### Caption correction

Import an episode with real names, acronyms, and cross-talk. Check whether you can fix text, timing, line breaks, and brand vocabulary before export. Animated caption styles are secondary if the transcript is wrong.

### Usage and exit costs

Credits may measure source minutes, generated output, or AI actions. Ask what happens when an episode is reprocessed, whether unused credits roll over, how long projects remain editable, whether bulk export costs more, and whether a clean MP4 is available. A low monthly price can become expensive if every revision consumes the full source again.

## OpusClip: best fit for automatic discovery and layout choice

OpusClip's official material emphasizes AI clipping, a vendor-defined Virality Score, animated captions, auto reframe, direct posting, and an editor on paid plans. Its layout documentation is unusually specific: Fill and Fit layouts, a two-speaker Split layout, three- and four-speaker layouts, screen-share and gameplay arrangements, plus manual reframe. The Split layout works only when both speakers appear together in the original frame, an important constraint that broad “multi-speaker” claims often omit.

Its current free plan lists 60 credits per month, rendered clips up to 1080p, auto reframe, and AI captions. Free clips have a watermark, cannot be edited, and stop being exportable after three days. Starter is listed at $15 monthly for 150 credits, editing, watermark removal, and posting to YouTube Shorts, TikTok, and Instagram Reels. Pro is listed at $29 monthly or $174 annually at the displayed annual offer, with 3,600 annual credits, two seats, multiple aspect ratios, scheduling, additional imports, and XML export to Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve.

**Trial it if:** your main job is finding and formatting multiple candidates from finished speech-led video, especially when you want several layout choices.

**Check before buying:** how often its selected moments match your show's editorial taste, how much boundary correction a typical clip needs, and whether your preferred input source sits on the selected plan.

## Vizard: best fit for batch repurposing and a manual fallback

Vizard documents automatic highlight detection, captions, ratios, auto reframe, a text-based editor, prompt search, and social publishing. Its help center also states that users can manually clip when automatic output is thin. That fallback matters for interviews with quiet but valuable explanations that a high-energy selector may overlook.

At the checked date, the official pricing page lists a free plan with 60 credits per month, 720p export, a full editor, one social account, and three-day storage. Vizard's help center explicitly says free exports carry a watermark. Paid Creator documentation lists watermark removal, 4K export, scheduling, and 100 GB of storage; Business adds a shared workspace and brand kit. The price values for paid plans did not render reliably in the captured official page, so we will not invent them. Verify the live checkout for your region and billing cycle.

Vizard says the number of generated clips varies with speech, source length, content suitability, and chosen duration. It recommends speech-led content over 15 minutes for stronger results and makes manual clipping available when the source does not yield enough automatic candidates.

**Trial it if:** you want a broad automatic batch but also expect to search the transcript and cut known moments manually in one project.

**Check before buying:** the current paid price, whether the speaker framing works on your camera composition, and how export/storage expiry affects your review schedule.

## Riverside: best fit from recording through repurposing

Riverside differs from dedicated post-production clippers because it begins with remote and in-person recording. Its pricing page documents separate tracks, editing, transcription, Magic Clips, podcast hosting, and publishing. That makes it attractive when the source is already recorded in Riverside and moving files into a second subscription creates more work than it saves.

The current free plan includes up to 720p video, a Riverside watermark, Magic Clips, and a one-off two-hour allowance for separate-track recordings. Pro lists up to 4K, no watermark, 15 hours of separate-track downloads per month, unlimited transcription, Magic Clips, editing, hosting, and publishing. The page shows $29 monthly or $24 per month when billed $288 annually. Magic Clips can identify highlights from recordings or uploaded files; current help documentation says Pro and Business users can generate additional clips and adjust duration, speaker focus, and keywords.

This is the strongest documented fit of the five when capture quality and isolated speaker tracks are part of the same purchasing decision. It may be more platform than a creator needs if all recordings arrive already edited from another studio.

**Trial it if:** your team wants to record guests, preserve separate tracks, edit the episode, generate clips, and host or publish without several handoffs.

**Check before buying:** whether collaborators need simultaneous editing, how separate-track hours match your recording volume, and whether its selected clips reduce enough manual review to justify an all-in-one plan.

## Descript: best fit for transcript-led precision

Descript treats the transcript as the main editing surface: delete or rearrange text and the media follows. Its current pricing lists Create Clips among the AI tools, and its prompt documentation gives a concrete example asking Underlord to turn a 60-minute podcast into five or six 30–60-second vertical clips with captions. The timeline remains available for exact boundaries.

Free currently includes 60 media minutes per month. Descript's help center says video exports from a Free drive include a bottom-right watermark. Hobbyist is listed at $16 per person/month with annual billing or $24 monthly, with ten media hours, 400 AI credits, 1080p watermark-free export, Underlord, and tools including Create Clips. Creator is listed at $24 annual-equivalent or $35 monthly, with 30 media hours, 800 AI credits, and 4K watermark-free export.

Descript's breadth is useful when clipping is one stage of a larger transcript, audio cleanup, show-note, and episode-editing process. That breadth also means AI credits and media minutes need separate attention. Its current help docs explain that media processing and AI actions draw from different pools, and some Underlord tasks have non-deterministic credit costs.

**Trial it if:** you want to correct the transcript, refine wording and pacing, and construct clips deliberately rather than depend on a large automated shortlist.

**Check before buying:** AI-credit consumption on your actual prompt pattern, caption and active-speaker behavior on multi-track projects, and whether transcript editing replaces enough timeline work.

## quso.ai: best fit for clipping plus social scheduling

quso.ai combines AI clip selection, vertical reframing, animated captions, editing, a brand kit, publishing, scheduling, and analytics. Its podcast clip generator says users can select layout, number of clips, and duration, then add subtitles, text, audio or video segments, emojis, backgrounds, and outros.

The current pricing page lists a Free plan with 75 credits per month, 720p rendering, chapters and short videos, TikTok publishing, and seven-day retention. Lite is listed at $29 monthly or $19 per month billed annually, with the annual display showing 200 credits, unlimited 1080p clips, three aspect ratios, ten GB storage, an advanced desktop editor, and publishing or scheduling to six platforms. Higher tiers add more credits, storage, content-planning, and brand controls.

Because quso.ai combines production and distribution, it is worth asking whether its calendar can replace a separate scheduler you already pay for. Do not count “one less subscription” as savings until the supported accounts, approval workflow, analytics, and retry behavior match your needs.

**Trial it if:** your bottleneck extends beyond editing into scheduling the approved clips across several channels.

**Check before buying:** free-export branding in the actual account, how credits are charged for retries, caption accuracy, and whether team approval is strong enough for your publishing risk.

## A 45-minute buying test

Do not trial a clip maker with the vendor's demo. Use one episode that contains two speakers, a proper name, a quiet teaching moment, crosstalk, and one section you know should not be quoted without context.

1. Mark six reference moments before upload.
2. Generate an automatic shortlist with default settings.
3. Count how many reference moments appear anywhere, not just at rank one.
4. Correct one missed moment through transcript, prompt, or timestamp controls.
5. Repair the boundaries and captions of the best two candidates.
6. Review every speaker change in vertical format.
7. Export a clean file and record the credits consumed, elapsed review time, watermark, and expiry date.

Run the same test in two products. The winner is the one that produces the lowest review cost while preserving meaning—not the one that creates the most thumbnails. Once you choose a tool, use the [podcast-to-clips production workflow](/blog/turn-a-podcast-into-short-video-clips) and keep a fixed approval checklist so speed does not erase context. Set the first batch with the [candidate-versus-approved clip yield method](/blog/how-many-clips-per-podcast-episode), not the number of thumbnails on the result page.
