## Evidence and quality checks

- **Intent:** The first two paragraphs give the requested dimensions and then immediately explain the broader classification rule. A reader can copy a practical preset without leaving with the false belief that 1080 x 1920 or exact 9:16 is YouTube's only accepted organic shape.
- **Original usefulness:** The platform-rule versus export-recommendation table solves the main contradiction in ranking results. The dimension-order examples, black-bar diagnosis, stale-claim table, and final-render checklist turn a spec page into an actionable editor handoff.
- **Length and classification:** YouTube's three-minute guidance still says square or vertical uploads up to three minutes are categorised as Shorts for standard channels when uploaded on or after 15 October 2024. It now gives 8 December 2025 as the equivalent transition date for Official Artist Channels. The article reports both dates accurately and notes that older uploads retain their existing classification.
- **Copyright condition:** The same official page still says a Short longer than one minute with an active Content ID claim of any type is blocked globally, cannot play or be recommended, and is not eligible for monetisation until resolved. The draft places this constraint next to the three-minute maximum rather than weakening it into a monetisation footnote.
- **Organic upload rule:** Current computer upload Help still says “up to three minutes” and “square or vertical.” It does not require `#Shorts`, an exact 9:16 ratio, or a single pixel size. The article uses those omissions carefully and does not claim that a hashtag affects file classification.
- **1080 x 1920 recommendation:** Google Ads' current video-view creative table recommends 1080 x 1920 for vertical Full HD and 9:16 for vertical assets. The article explicitly labels that as corroborating export guidance, not the organic Shorts rule.
- **Encoding:** YouTube's current encoding page supports MP4, H.264 High Profile, progressive scan, variable bitrate, AAC-LC or other listed audio codecs, 48 kHz, preserving the recorded frame rate, and BT.709 for ordinary SDR uploads. The supported-format page separately confirms MOV, MP4, WebM, and the broader accepted list. The draft therefore recommends a dependable path without saying it is the only valid path.
- **Canvas and player behaviour:** YouTube's upload guidance still says the player adapts to vertical, square, and horizontal files and advises creators not to add black bars to the sides of vertical video. The article's rendered-property inspection follows directly from that distinction.
- **Safe-zone restraint:** The device and interface advice is qualitative and appropriately cautious. Community reports motivate checking edge content, but no Reddit post is converted into an official pixel measurement or crop percentage.
- **Product truth:** The final Vidrial paragraph promises only authorised-source moment finding, caption correction, and editable clips before export. It does not claim automatic subject tracking, multi-speaker layouts, B-roll, or SRT/VTT sidecar export.
- **Internal links:** Both related article slugs and `/youtube-clipper` exist. Their anchors accurately describe the separate selection and caption jobs.
- **Corpus:** This is the only page whose primary job is a current specification sheet. Article 12 owns automation and article 14 owns caption delivery; the unavoidable references to crop and caption placement do not duplicate their procedures. The corpus audit reports no overlap finding.
- **Naturalness:** The draft has a direct answer, varied section shapes, and visible editorial judgment. It avoids false certainty, keyword stuffing, generic scene-setting, and a future-facing recap.
- **Metadata:** Frontmatter validates; the title, description, summaries, FAQs, sources, dates, and ten-minute reading time match the body.
