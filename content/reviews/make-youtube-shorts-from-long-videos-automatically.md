## Evidence and quality checks

- **Intent:** The first paragraph answers the automation query and identifies the reader's real decision. It does not claim that YouTube itself selects the strongest excerpts or that an external clipper safely finishes and publishes them without review.
- **Original usefulness:** The automation-boundary table, four-part search brief, context envelope, source-range record, and `candidate → editing → review → approved → scheduled → published` states give a small team a reusable operating method. The hypothetical onboarding example shows why qualifiers outside an AI-selected range matter.
- **Native YouTube route:** Current YouTube Help still says **Edit into a Short** is for a public long-form video uploaded by the channel owner, selects up to 60 seconds, links the result back to the source, and is unavailable for private, unlisted, third-party-claimed, or sampling-disabled videos. It also says Audio Library music cannot be used in a Short created through this specific route. The article keeps those restrictions together.
- **Current upload rules:** YouTube's computer instructions still accept up to three minutes with a square or vertical aspect ratio and allow up to 15 files to be selected before their draft details are completed. The separate three-minute page still says an over-one-minute Short with any active Content ID claim is blocked globally. The article dates and distinguishes these limits from the 60-second native selection.
- **External clipper evidence:** Descript currently documents 1–20 generated clips, prompt guidance by topic, goal, or criteria, portrait and square layouts, and separately editable clip compositions. The article uses that source only to support a candidate-generation workflow; it does not repeat Descript's “most engaging” language as proof that a machine found the best or viral moment.
- **Encoding:** Current YouTube guidance supports MP4, H.264, AAC-LC or other listed audio paths, progressive video, and preserving the recorded frame rate. The article presents MP4/H.264 as a practical route, not the only accepted file type.
- **Rights and limitations:** Ownership, participant permissions, music rights, high-stakes context, crop failures, transcript errors, and final-render inspection appear next to the convenience of automation. No universal clips-per-hour ratio or outcome claim is invented.
- **Product truth:** The Available Vidrial list matches `src/domain/features/availability.ts` and the YouTube connector registry: eligible rights-attested YouTube ingestion, discovery and prompt search, complete-thought/hook/clarity signals, transcript editing, caption correction, timeline rearrangement, MP4 export, and separately authorised YouTube publishing/scheduling. Animated caption presets and long-silence removal are correctly labelled Beta. Subject tracking, multi-speaker layouts, filler-word removal, B-roll, and SRT/VTT export are correctly labelled Coming soon.
- **Internal links:** The dimensions and caption targets exist and are themselves reviewed in this batch. `/youtube-clipper` uses canonical product language and does not promise planned sidecar-caption export or automatic subject tracking.
- **Corpus:** Article 11 owns the two basic routes, native eligibility failures, link-back mechanics, and general YouTube edit. Article 2 owns a platform-broader AI clip repair workflow. This page remains distinct through its automation job map, brief, context envelope, batch states, and release boundary. The corpus audit reports no heading-template or phrase-overlap finding.
- **Naturalness:** The opening is answer-first, paragraph lengths vary, and the draft uses specific editorial judgments rather than inflated adjectives. It has no fake test, invented statistic, generic “video is important” preamble, or repetitive conclusion.
